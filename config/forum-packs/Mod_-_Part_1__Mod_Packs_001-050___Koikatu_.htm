Regarding your first question: While cloud platforms like Gofile are indeed capable of handling large batches of small files, I choose to compress them into archives and distribute them across multiple anonymous hosting services to avoid upload instability. Mass-uploading individual files introduces many uncontrollable factors, often resulting in broken or failed transfers; therefore, bundling them is much more efficient for organization.

If you examine the file lists carefully, you will notice that the mods are sorted alphabetically by GUID, which should assist in locating specific files. If I were to upload every file individually, it would expose the full filename structure, which I feel is discourteous to the original creators—especially since my collection includes some very recent content. While I am not entirely sure if it is possible to extract specific files directly from within the archives without downloading the whole package, this method is primarily for the sake of anonymity and upload convenience. For users with a home connection and unlimited bandwidth, downloading the full pack and simply deleting what you don’t need is a viable workaround.

Regarding your second point: Thank you for the reminder; I will certainly look into that. You have a great eye for detail, and I truly appreciate your kindness.

The issue is caused by a filename encoding mismatch. The zip files were likely created on a Japanese system using Shift-JIS encoding, but Pixeldrain uses UTF-8.
When you try to download a single file, the server can't parse the Japanese characters correctly (showing them as garbled text), so it fails to locate the file path. However, downloading the whole archive works because it transfers the raw binary data without needing to parse individual filenames.
